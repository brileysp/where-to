# What counts as an All-inclusive Resort (proposal for the owner, 25 Oct 2026)

Question from the owner on the column proposal: how do we define an all-inclusive resort, and where do African luxury lodges fall?

**Proposed rule.** An all-inclusive resort for this interest is a property where (1) one rate covers the room, meals, drinks and on-site activities, **and** (2) the property itself fills the days: pool or beach, activities, shows, several restaurants and bars. Test: if you never left the property, would the week still be a full holiday?

**Africa's luxury lodges** are priced all-inclusive (meals, drinks and game drives) but fail the test: the draw is the wildlife off the property, and the stay is built around excursions. They belong to Safari, and to Luxury Hotels' "Remote luxury lodges" chip. The same holds for wilderness camps, fishing lodges and expedition ships.

**In:** Caribbean and Mexican resort chains, Canary and Balearic all-inclusive hotels, Indian Ocean and Maldives island resorts, Red Sea resorts, Club Med style villages.
**Out:** safari lodges, wilderness camps, lodges where the activities are the sold product, cruises.
**Borderline (decided by the test):** Maldives island resorts and some Fijian and Seychelles resorts are all-inclusive and self-contained, so they are in; a small lodge that includes a guided daily activity as its main offer is out.
